## G3.1: Fair local odds, only three possible worlds
- Family / paper: 113 — Entropy and Face Dimension of the Perfect-Matching Polytope — `preprints/Entropy-and-Face-Dimension-of-the-Perfect-Matching-Polytope-September-23-2026/build/sections/10-examples.tex`
- Quote: "Although an expansion can introduce further perfect matchings, the prescribed uniform edge marginals force a law to remain on three of them."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Represent paired rooms, NPCs, or rewards by perfect matchings, and require every permitted edge to appear with probability exactly 1/3. Start with K4 and its three edge-disjoint perfect matchings; replace a degree-three vertex by a triangle, reconnect its three old edges to separate triangle vertices, and extend each inherited matching with the triangle edge opposite its selected old edge. Repeating this construction grows the graph, but the prescribed local odds still force a unique uniform distribution on the three inherited matchings. This gives a procedural-generation diagnostic: increasing the map size can leave global variety fixed even when every individual edge looks fair.
- Demo sketch: Render the paper's eight-vertex graph with edges `01,03,05,14,16,23,24,27,34,56,57,67`, then animate further triangle expansions. WITH the face restriction, sample the three inherited matchings uniformly; WITHOUT, sample all five perfect matchings of the eight-vertex graph uniformly, which violates the requested edge odds. Measure maximum marginal error (exactly 4/15 for that baseline), distinct outcomes, generation time, and joint entropy: the constrained law has H = ln(3), while the invalid coefficient-one estimate F-B = 4 ln(4/3) overstates it by ln(256/243).
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves that the prescribed-marginal law is unique, with H = ln(3), F = m ln(3), and B = 2m ln(3/2) on graphs with 2m vertices; triangle expansion itself is an established construction reused by the paper. The game application is speculative, and the three-outcome restriction concerns laws with the specified marginals, not the total number of perfect matchings; the small instance's five matchings and baseline error were independently checked by enumeration.

## G3.2: Morph convex rooms without collapsing their volume
- Family / paper: 091 — The logarithmic Brunn–Minkowski conjecture — `preprints/The-logarithmic-Brunn-Minkowski-conjecture-September-23-2026/build/main.tex`; construction and theorem checked in `preprints/The-logarithmic-Brunn-Minkowski-conjecture-September-23-2026/build/introduction.tex`
- Quote: "We prove the logarithmic Brunn--Minkowski conjecture for arbitrary origin-symmetric convex bodies in every dimension."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Morph two origin-symmetric convex rooms by interpolating directional support bounds geometrically: h_lambda(u) = h_K(u)^(1-lambda) h_L(u)^lambda. Construct the intermediate room as the intersection of the half-spaces u·x <= h_lambda(u), rather than treating those bounds as an already valid mesh or support function. The manuscript's volume floor is |K_lambda| >= |K|^(1-lambda) |L|^lambda, so equal-volume endpoints cannot produce a smaller intermediate room. Arbitrarily oriented symmetric bodies in three dimensions give a useful case beyond the previously established planar setting.
- Demo sketch: A small C/C++ mesh viewer clips a convex polyhedron against interpolated half-spaces, using both endpoint meshes' facet normals plus extra paired sample directions. WITH the support-bound construction, show the moving room and its volume floor; WITHOUT, linearly interpolate matched vertices of the same endpoints. Measure volume divided by the geometric endpoint-volume floor, minimum volume during the morph, changes as direction sampling is refined, and milliseconds per update.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves the continuum volume inequality for full-dimensional origin-symmetric convex bodies, not arbitrary nonconvex rooms or a collision solver. Sampled half-space intersections contain the continuum Wulff body and therefore retain its lower volume floor in exact arithmetic, but their shape accuracy and floating-point clipping need separate checks; using the construction for level morphing is an extrapolation.
- Wow: high · Confidence: high · Effort: M

## G3.3: A crystal seed certified for an entire class of repulsions
- Family / paper: 090 — Universal optimality of the triangular lattice — `preprints/Universal-optimality-of-the-triangular-lattice-September-23-2026/build/main.tex`; lattice and energy definitions checked in `preprints/Universal-optimality-of-the-triangular-lattice-September-23-2026/build/sections/01-uniform-gaussian-theorem.tex`
- Quote: "We prove universal energy minimality for the triangular lattice in the plane."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Generate a density-one particle or placement lattice directly with b = sqrt(3)/2 and positions b^(-1/2) (j+k/2, kb). The manuscript claims this same arrangement minimizes energy per particle for every nonnegative completely monotone potential of squared distance, including Gaussian repulsions and positive mixtures of them, against nonlattice competitors as well as other lattices. A particle sandbox can switch interaction range or mixture weights while retaining the same certified thermodynamic target. The lattice generator is classical; the new contribution is the breadth of the energy comparison.
- Demo sketch: Use 256 particles in a periodic rhombic cell and a Gaussian pair potential, evaluating periodic images with a recorded tail cutoff. WITH the theorem-guided seed, initialize the triangular lattice directly; WITHOUT, initialize random or square-grid placements and run the same relaxation update. Show crystalline order and defects, and measure energy per particle, relaxation steps to a chosen energy tolerance, initialization time, and milliseconds per step.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves a lower-limit energy comparison for infinite configurations of centered disk density one; it does not prove gradient-descent convergence, finite open-boundary optimality, or a stable timestep. Periodically repeated configurations fit that comparison when their full image interactions are included, while the finite image cutoff and proposed settling-time benefit remain numerical questions.
- Wow: med · Confidence: high · Effort: S

## G3.4: An exact noise budget for an NPC choosing its best action
- Family / paper: 096 — The Gaussian propeller bound in every dimension — `preprints/The-Gaussian-Propeller-Bound-in-Every-Dimension-September-24-2026/build/main.tex`, Gaussian-maxima corollary and attaining-propeller construction
- Quote: "The constant is sharp already for three vectors in the plane."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Give an NPC correlated Gaussian action scores X_i = v_i·g, and normalize its centered score budget E = sum_i ||v_i-v_bar||^2. The paper gives the explicit ceiling E[max_i X_i] <= 3 sqrt(E)/(2 sqrt(2 pi)), regardless of the number of actions or Gaussian dimensions. Three equal-length score vectors spaced 120 degrees apart attain that ceiling; with E = 1, each has length 1/sqrt(3), and their winning regions are three planar sectors. This is a concrete way to compare action sets under the same noise budget instead of assuming that adding more actions always increases the expected best score.
- Demo sketch: Generate Gaussian samples with Box–Muller and display winning actions as colored regions, alongside the action-vector geometry. WITH the sharp construction, use the three equilateral vectors at E = 1; WITHOUT, use normalized random or orthogonal action vectors with 4, 8, or 16 labels at the same budget. Measure mean winning score, its gap to the explicit ceiling, action frequencies, and milliseconds per batch, projecting higher-dimensional samples into a plane only for display.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves the first-moment partition bound 9/(8 pi) in every dimension and derives this sharp expected-maximum inequality, including singular Gaussian covariances. The application to NPC tuning is speculative: it is an expectation bound under Gaussian scores, not a per-frame score cap, a guarantee of balanced action frequencies, or a result for arbitrary noise distributions.
- Wow: med · Confidence: high · Effort: S

## G3.5: XOR interactions with a fixed budget across scales
- Family / paper: 082 — An L³ bound for the dyadic triangular Hilbert form — `preprints/An-L3-bound-for-the-dyadic-triangular-Hilbert-form-October-5-2026/build/main.tex`; finite-array formula checked in `preprints/An-L3-bound-for-the-dyadic-triangular-Hilbert-form-October-5-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex` and `preprints/An-L3-bound-for-the-dyadic-triangular-Hilbert-form-October-5-2026/build/sections/04-dyadic.tex`
- Quote: "The sum of the absolute local contributions over any finite set of scales is bounded by forty times the product of the input norms."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Let three real fields encode cyclic pair interactions F0(x0,x1), F1(x1,x2), and F2(x2,x0). At interval length 2^k, use triples whose interval indices obey n0 XOR n1 XOR n2 = 0, and integrate the product of the fields with each interval's left/right Haar sign, multiplied by 2^(-k). The claimed budget is sum |L_I| <= 40 ||F0||_3 ||F1||_3 ||F2||_3 across any finite scale selection, even when every local contribution receives its own coefficient of magnitude at most one. That supplies a bounded scalar interaction score for a cyclic-compatibility puzzle or a drawable hierarchy of positive and negative contributions.
- Demo sketch: Use three editable 32×32 piecewise-constant fields and show each scale's contributions as colored blocks; the paper's finite-array model permits direct weighted sums without a continuum approximation. WITH the XOR/Haar construction, include the alternating signs; WITHOUT, keep the same admissible triples and normalization but replace all Haar signs by +1 to expose repeated counting of broad correlated structure. Plot accumulated absolute contribution divided by the product of the L3 norms, number of scales, and milliseconds per recomputation; do not assume the baseline must grow without bound.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves the scalar absolute-sum estimate for unrestricted real L3 inputs, with an explicit constant 40 and no astronomical parameter choice. Using the score in gameplay is speculative: the theorem supplies neither a pointwise bound for a rendered field nor stability of a simulation that feeds the score back into its evolving inputs.

## G3.6: Signed texture filtering with a turning-scale knob
- Family / paper: 083 — A uniform Hilbert transform estimate for Lipschitz directions — `preprints/A-uniform-Hilbert-transform-estimate-for-Lipschitz-directions-September-25-2026/build/main.tex`; operator and scaling checked in `preprints/A-uniform-Hilbert-transform-estimate-for-Lipschitz-directions-September-25-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex`
- Quote: "The bound is uniform in the inner truncation and holds for fields depending on both coordinates."
- Kind: visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Reveal signed texture edges along a smoothly varying direction field with Hf(x) = integral_epsilon^a [f(x-tv(x))-f(x+tv(x))]/t dt. Each pixel samples a straight line with direction frozen at that pixel, rather than following a curved streamline. The paper claims a uniform continuum L2 gain bound at outer length a = a_*/Lip(v), giving a reciprocal-turning scale to explore when designing the filter. For theta(x,y) = A sin(kx x+ky y) and v = (cos(theta),sin(theta)), the computable upper bound Lip(v) <= |A| sqrt(kx^2+ky^2) gives a conservative length parameter.
- Demo sketch: Use a compactly supported smooth texture and direction field on a padded raster, with paired logarithmically spaced samples on each frozen line. WITH the proposed scaling, set a = kappa/L using that Lipschitz upper bound; WITHOUT, hold a fixed while increasing A or the spatial frequencies. Display the filtered texture and measure empirical L2 gain, changes as the inner cutoff and raster spacing shrink, and milliseconds per frame.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper asserts existence of absolute a_* in (0,1/2) and finite C_*, with the supremum over inner cutoffs outside the L2 norm, but gives no numerical values for either constant. Consequently a chosen kappa is an empirical experiment, not a certified threshold, and neither raster stability nor failure of the fixed-length baseline follows from the continuum theorem.
- Wow: med · Confidence: med · Effort: S
