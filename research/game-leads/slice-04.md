## G4.1: Randomize dungeon graphs without changing room degrees

- Family / paper: 131 — Polynomial mixing of the switch chain for every graphical degree sequence — `preprints/Polynomial-Mixing-of-the-Switch-Chain-for-Every-Graphical-Degree-Sequence-September-25-2026/main.pdf`. Source checked: `preprints/Polynomial-Mixing-of-the-Switch-Chain-for-Every-Graphical-Degree-Sequence-September-25-2026/build/sections/setup.tex` and `preprints/Polynomial-Mixing-of-the-Switch-Chain-for-Every-Graphical-Degree-Sequence-September-25-2026/build/sections/mixing.tex`.
- Quote: "Every such reassignment preserves all degrees and simplicity; there are no further constraints on the subset."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Generate dungeon, trade, or social graphs with an exact prescribed number of links at every labeled vertex, then randomize the topology by undirected Curveball trades. Choose a vertex pair uniformly; collect outside vertices adjacent to exactly one endpoint, then uniformly reassign the same number to the first endpoint, preserving the pair's shared neighbors and mutual edge. The paper proves a spectral inequality for this whole-neighborhood resampling, which gives a much more usable mixing guarantee than its headline single-switch bound. Connectedness, geometric edge limits, and forbidden host edges are additional constraints outside this guarantee.
- Demo sketch: WITH the math, animate pair trades from a fixed degree realization; WITHOUT baseline, run the paper's lazy single-edge-switch chain from the same graph and compare at equal elapsed time. For six vertices all of degree two, enumerate the 70 possible graphs and plot empirical total-variation distance from uniform; for larger graphs show degree error, edge autocorrelation, changed edges per millisecond, and milliseconds per sample. With an adjacency matrix, a trade needs an O(n) scan and an unbiased shuffle of at most n-2 singleton vertices.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves Var_pi(f) <= <f,Hf> for H = sum_a(I-E_a); for B = binom(n,2), the derived kernel K = B^(-1)sum_a E_a is positive semidefinite with spectral gap at least 1/B, giving TV <= 1/4 after ceil[B(1+B/2) ln 2] trades, or 5,074 at n=16. This O(n^4)-trade corollary is derived here from the checked proof, while the headline theorem is 2n^8 single-switch steps; practical speed and suitability for dungeon layouts remain speculative, and the separate exactly uniform sampler uses enumeration and is excluded from the demo.
- Wow: high · Confidence: high · Effort: M

## G4.2: An oscillator that wakes only after an amplitude kick

- Family / paper: 143 — Two limit cycles for quintic Liénard systems — `preprints/two-limit-cycles-for-quintic-lienard-systems-September-24-2026/two-limit-cycles-for-quintic-lienard-systems-September-24-2026.pdf`. Source checked: `preprints/two-limit-cycles-for-quintic-lienard-systems-September-24-2026/build/sections/introduction.tex` and `preprints/two-limit-cycles-for-quintic-lienard-systems-September-24-2026/build/sections/sharpness.tex`.
- Quote: "Thus both cycles are hyperbolic: the inner one repels and the outer one attracts."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Use xdot = y-F(x), ydot = -x with F(x) = epsilon(4x - 20x^3/3 + 8x^5/5). For sufficiently small positive epsilon, the paper constructs a repelling cycle near amplitude one and an attracting cycle near amplitude two; the linearization also makes the origin attracting. A player-controlled impulse can therefore move an otherwise resting oscillator across a boundary into persistent motion, useful for reactive creatures, engines, or pulsing visual effects. The new global result caps the number of isolated cycles at two even when arbitrary even coefficients are added, provided F remains degree at most five and the restoring force remains x.
- Demo sketch: WITH the construction, integrate a grid of initial states and draw the rest basin, unstable boundary, attracting loop, and an impulse-controlled particle; WITHOUT baseline, use the ordinary cubic van der Pol oscillator, whose stable oscillation lacks this rest-to-oscillation basin gate. Measure first-return displacement, cycle amplitudes and periods, step-halving error, and milliseconds per integration batch; locate the repelling cycle through the return map rather than waiting for forward trajectories to settle onto it. The paper supplies no numeric epsilon cutoff, so an initial trial such as epsilon=0.01 needs numerical return-map and timestep checks before claiming that it displays both cycles.
- Proven vs. speculative: The manuscript proves at most two geometrically distinct isolated periodic orbits for every real F of degree at most five and constructs the two hyperbolic cycles for sufficiently small epsilon. The impulse mechanic and finite-step rendering are extrapolations; the construction itself is classical, and the general two-cycle cap is the new design certificate.
- Wow: high · Confidence: high · Effort: S

## G4.3: Fractal density that counts overlapping maps correctly

- Family / paper: 148 — The entropy-rate dimension formula for self-similar measures on the line — `preprints/The-entropy-rate-dimension-formula-for-self-similar-measures-on-the-line-September-24-2026/main.pdf`. Source checked: `preprints/The-entropy-rate-dimension-formula-for-self-similar-measures-on-the-line-September-24-2026/build/sections/introduction.tex` and `preprints/The-entropy-rate-dimension-formula-for-self-similar-measures-on-the-line-September-24-2026/build/sections/corollaries.tex`.
- Quote: "Exact overlaps are allowed, and the contraction ratios may be unequal and negative."
- Kind: visualization | gameplay | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Give an iterated-function-system texture or resource-spawn distribution a density readout based on distinct composed affine maps, rather than treating every random address as new information. The theorem gives dim_H(mu) = min(1,h_RW/chi), with chi = -sum_i p_i log|r_i| and h_RW = lim H(G_m)/m. A concrete illustrative system is the three equally weighted maps x/3, x/3+1, x/3+3: the words 02 and 10 define exactly the same map. Its naive address-entropy estimate is dimension one, while the depth-two map entropy already gives the rigorous upper bound 1 - 1/(9 log_2 3), approximately 0.92990.
- Demo sketch: WITH the formula, draw the sampled density as a scrolling strip and merge equal rational affine maps before computing H(G_m)/m; WITHOUT baseline, display H(p)/chi, which stays at one for this example. Enumerate modest depths with integer translation keys using N_next = 3N + digit for digit in {0,1,3}, and compare the entropy readout with histogram-entropy slopes over several pixel scales. Measure collisions, the dimension upper bound, finite-scale slope discrepancy, milliseconds per entropy update, and rendering fps.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves the exact limiting dimension formula without separation; the three-map example and its depth-two bound are calculations supplied here, while the texture/spawn use is speculative. Finite-depth H(G_m)/m gives an upper bound without a certified convergence rate, and dimension one alone does not imply absolute continuity, uniform density, or smooth-looking pixels.
- Wow: high · Confidence: high · Effort: S

## G4.4: A graph rule that keeps reaction economies alive and bounded

- Family / paper: 149 — Uniform Permanence in Weakly Reversible Mass-Action Systems — `preprints/Uniform-Permanence-in-Weakly-Reversible-Mass-Action-Systems-October-5-2026/permanence.pdf`. Source checked: `preprints/Uniform-Permanence-in-Weakly-Reversible-Mass-Action-Systems-October-5-2026/build/sections/introduction.tex`, `preprints/Uniform-Permanence-in-Weakly-Reversible-Mass-Action-Systems-October-5-2026/build/sections/trapping.tex`, and `preprints/Uniform-Permanence-in-Weakly-Reversible-Mass-Action-Systems-October-5-2026/build/sections/permanence.tex`.
- Quote: "The set depends only on the network, the rates, and the class; the entry time may depend on the initial state."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Build a continuous crafting economy, chemical garden, or population toy from reactions y -> y' with rates k*x^y and strictly positive starting quantities. Require every directed reaction to have a directed return path, a simple reachability check on the graph of complexes. For fixed positive rates, this rule guarantees eventual positive lower and finite upper concentrations throughout each fixed conservation class, including unbounded classes and networks with several linkage classes. It gives a structural filter for procedurally generated networks without requiring balanced fluxes at a chosen equilibrium.
- Demo sketch: WITH the rule, generate a small network with several strongly connected linkage classes and integrate xdot = sum k*x^y*(y'-y) for many positive initial states in the same conservation class; WITHOUT baseline, show an irreversible autocatalytic control A -> 2A, whose concentration grows exponentially. Draw reaction fluxes and concentration bars, and report minimum/maximum concentration over time, conservation-law residual, step-halving error, and milliseconds per step. A small C/C++ implementation needs only integer complex vectors, a reaction list, graph reachability, and an ODE integrator.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves a common compact convex forward-invariant absorbing set for each class, with entry times allowed to depend on the initial state; its trapping proof does not supply convenient numeric bounds or an efficient general bound calculator. The gameplay bridge is speculative and applies to continuous mass-action equations with fixed positive rates, with no guarantee for integer inventories, stochastic extinction, player-driven rate changes, or an arbitrary numerical timestep.
- Wow: med · Confidence: high · Effort: S

## G4.5: A sharp ceiling for one-bit clues in a noisy world

- Family / paper: 119 — Sharp binary-information contraction on the discrete cube — `preprints/Sharp-binary-information-contraction-on-the-discrete-cube-September-24-2026/main.pdf`. Source checked: `preprints/Sharp-binary-information-contraction-on-the-discrete-cube-September-24-2026/build/main.tex`.
- Quote: "At fixed initial information, a noisy coordinate retains the most information."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Create a sensor puzzle where a device stores one bit about independent fair world bits, after which every world bit flips independently with probability q. The theorem gives the maximum information any Boolean one-bit summary can retain about the entire noisy world: 1-h_2(q) bits, attained by storing a single coordinate. Compare coordinate, majority, parity, and random balanced clues against this exact ceiling, separating information retained from the clue's usefulness for a particular objective.
- Demo sketch: WITH the result, enumerate n=9 inputs, calculate the noisy posterior by nine passes of two-entry averaging, and show the coordinate clue touching the proved ceiling; WITHOUT baseline, use majority, parity, or a random balanced truth table with the same inputs and noise. Render posterior certainty as colored tiles and measure retained bits, distance below the ceiling, prediction error, and milliseconds per noise-slider update. The calculation is O(n*2^n), practical at this demo size but unsuitable as a general large-dimension game-loop routine.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves sharp contraction for randomized binary summaries at fixed initial information, including the Boolean ceiling and an output-bias refinement. The puzzle is speculative; the theorem assumes a uniform clean vector and independent symmetric noise, and does not say that one coordinate is best for repeated observations of a shared hidden fact or for a semantically prescribed alert.
- Wow: med · Confidence: high · Effort: S

## G4.6: Polynomial degree as a bound on decision flicker

- Family / paper: 127 — Average sensitivity of polynomial threshold functions — `preprints/Average-Sensitivity-of-Polynomial-Threshold-Functions-September-25-2026/main.pdf`. Source checked: `preprints/Average-Sensitivity-of-Polynomial-Threshold-Functions-September-25-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex` and `preprints/Average-Sensitivity-of-Polynomial-Threshold-Functions-September-25-2026/build/sections/06-consequences.tex`.
- Quote: "The constant is independent of both $d$ and $n$."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Use the sign of a degree-d polynomial in n binary sensors as an NPC decision gate. The proved influence bound implies reversal probability at most min(1,8d/sqrt(n)) for a uniformly random state and one uniformly selected bit flip; independent flips of rate eta also have the bound min(1,8*sqrt(2)*d*sqrt(eta)). The paper gives an explicit drawable gate p_J(x) = product_{j in J}(t(x)-j-1/2), where t is the number of positive sensors and J consists of d indices nearest (n-1)/2. Its alternating Hamming-weight layers make degree and decision changes easy to see.
- Demo sketch: WITH the degree restriction, animate uniformly initialized sensor vectors using this gate; WITHOUT baseline, use full parity on the same vectors and perturbations, which reverses on every single-bit flip. Maintain the Hamming count so the explicit gate costs O(d) per single-bit update, then display decision colors, reversal fraction, measured average sensitivity, the theoretical ceiling, and nanoseconds per decision. Include a large-n setting: the single-bit bound becomes nontrivial only when n>64d^2, and the independent-noise bound needs correspondingly small eta.
- Proven vs. speculative: The manuscript proves influence at most 8d*sqrt(n), uniformly in both parameters, with sign(0)=1; its consequences section derives the independent-noise constant 8*sqrt(2). NPC robustness is an extrapolation under these input/noise laws, with no state-by-state, correlated-sensor, or adversarial guarantee; the layered gate is a classical example, while the uniform bound is the new result.
- Wow: med · Confidence: high · Effort: S
