## G6.1: An exact roughness knob for constrained random surfaces

- Family / paper: 225 — The Gaussian free field limit of the balanced six-vertex model with variance multiplier 1/arcsin(c/2) — `preprints/The-Gaussian-free-field-limit-of-the-balanced-six-vertex-model-with-variance-multiplier-1-over-arcsin-c-over-2-September-23-2026/The-Gaussian-free-field-limit-of-the-balanced-six-vertex-model-with-variance-multiplier-1-over-arcsin-c-over-2-September-23-2026.pdf`. Opened source: `preprints/The-Gaussian-free-field-limit-of-the-balanced-six-vertex-model-with-variance-multiplier-1-over-arcsin-c-over-2-September-23-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex`.
- Quote: "Its height function is a discrete random surface."
- Kind: visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Make a surface from square-lattice arrows with exactly two arrows entering and two leaving each vertex; the two vertices with both horizontal arrows inward or outward have weight c, and the other four have weight 1. For fixed 0 < c ≤ 2 and unit height jumps, the large-scale field has squared Gaussian multiplier σ²(c) = 1/arcsin(c/2), with Green kernel −log|x−y|/(2π). This supplies a calibrated roughness control across the full stated regime, including c = 2, rather than an amplitude chosen by eye.
- Demo sketch: In C/C++, sample balanced periodic arrow configurations using reversible directed-plaquette flips with acceptance min(1, c^ΔNc), reconstruct heights, and render a smoothed surface. WITH the result, multiply heights by sqrt(arcsin(c/2)) to compare normalized coarse statistics across c; WITHOUT, keep the same raw height units for every c. Measure covariance error for smooth zero-mean spatial tests, residual amplitude variation across c, acceptance rate, and ms/sweep.
- Proven vs. speculative: The manuscript proves the exact multiplier and Gaussian free field convergence for the plane state obtained by first sending one balanced-torus dimension to infinity, then the other. Finite-torus sampling, adequate burn-in, and terrain use are extrapolations: there is no mixing guarantee, usable finite-size rate, or bound uniform as c approaches zero, and the continuum field requires smoothing for display.
- Wow: high · Confidence: med · Effort: M

## G6.2: A fractal frontier whose animation clock survives refinement

- Family / paper: 223 — Natural Occupation Measures for Critical Square-Lattice FK Interfaces — `preprints/Natural-Occupation-Measures-for-Critical-Square-Lattice-FK-Interfaces-October-5-2026/natural-occupation-measures-critical-square-lattice-fk-interfaces.pdf`. Opened source: `preprints/Natural-Occupation-Measures-for-Critical-Square-Lattice-FK-Interfaces-October-5-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex`.
- Quote: "A single deterministic constant times the predicted power of the mesh gives the normalization."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Animate a territory frontier or growing crack whose total traversal time has a consistent large-scale distribution as resolution changes. For fixed 1 ≤ q < 4, sample critical FK edges with p = sqrt(q)/(1+sqrt(q)), use the paper's wired/free boundary arcs and medial turn pairings, and set κ(q) = 4π/arccos(−sqrt(q)/2) and d(q) = 1+κ(q)/8. If δ is the mesh and Nδ the number of interface traversals, c(q)δ^d Nδ converges to the curve's Minkowski content jointly with its shape; the new conclusion supplies occupation and total-step scaling as well as geometry.
- Demo sketch: Start with q = 1, where the sampled edges are independent fair bits, and trace interfaces on 64², 128², and 256² grids. WITH the result, give each traversal a duration proportional to δ^(7/4); WITHOUT, use fixed time per traversal or Euclidean-length time proportional to δ. Compare total-duration distributions and occupation in fixed screen bins across resolutions, alongside sample/trace milliseconds and rendering FPS.
- Proven vs. speculative: The manuscript proves joint convergence of the ordered curve and spatial counting measure, including total mass, for the specified square and boundary conventions. Using that measure as an animation clock is speculative; c(q) is positive but not numerically evaluated, convergence rates are unspecified, and the statement excludes q = 4 and uniformity as q approaches 4.

## G6.3: Keep random-map connectivity difficulty consistent across resolutions

- Family / papers: 224 — From critical crossings to quenched near-critical universality in Voronoi percolation — `preprints/From-critical-crossings-to-quenched-near-critical-universality-in-Voronoi-percolation-October-5-2026/critical-crossings-quenched-near-critical-universality-voronoi-percolation.pdf`; A Pivotal Amplitude for Voronoi Percolation from Cardy's Formula — `preprints/A-Pivotal-Amplitude-for-Voronoi-Percolation-from-Cardys-Formula-October-5-2026/voronoi-pivotal-amplitude.pdf`. Opened sources: the first manuscript's `build/sections/01-introduction.tex` and the second's `build/sections/00-introduction.tex`.
- Quote: "Each model is normalized by its own expected number of color-pivotal sites for a unit-square crossing."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Treat independently colored Voronoi cells as traversable terrain and use a dimensionless connectivity slider λ. Assign each cell one uniform mark U and open it when U ≤ p(λ), where p(λ) = clip(1/2 + λ/E[Npivotal]); the expectation averages both geometry and critical colors. The papers identify a common near-critical crossing-threshold law with triangular percolation and establish E[Npivotal] ~ cV δ^(−3/4), so the sensitivity window scales as δ^(3/4) instead of a resolution-independent probability increment.
- Demo sketch: Generate a Poisson–Voronoi board with a guard region around the displayed square and a triangular reference board, then estimate each mean pivotal count by repeated color-flip crossing tests. WITH the result, sweep λ using each board's measured pivotal normalization; WITHOUT, apply the same fixed Δp slider at every resolution. Measure crossing-probability curves, differences between threshold distributions, spread across tessellations, and ms/crossing test, while checking guard-region and geometric-discretization effects separately.
- Proven vs. speculative: Assuming the stated critical Cardy input, the transfer theorem proves convergence of joint threshold laws conditional on the tessellation, in probability over tessellations; the amplitude theorem proves a positive limiting coefficient rather than just an exponent. Game difficulty calibration and small-board agreement remain experiments, since cV is not numerically supplied, finite-size rates are absent, and a finite guard region approximates the full-plane tessellation.
- Wow: high · Confidence: med · Effort: M

## G6.4: Terrain noise with exact covariance bands

- Family / paper: 216 — BKT universality for height and planar spin fields — `preprints/BKT-universality-for-height-and-planar-spin-fields-September-24-2026/paper.pdf`. Opened source: `preprints/BKT-universality-for-height-and-planar-spin-fields-September-24-2026/build/sections/toolkit.tex`, Lemma “Finite-range decomposition”; the introduction was also checked.
- Quote: "It has range at most $2pS$."
- Kind: visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Build a procedural height field from independent Gaussian bands, letting the designer retain the broad landscape while regenerating finer detail. With p = 32, γ = 1/16, S = Lm, L a power of two, m = 1,L,L²,..., and |s| ≤ 1/4, the explicit positive band multiplier is Γm(λ) = (1−γλ)[Qm(1−λ/16)−QS(1−λ/16)]/[(1+s)λ], where Qt(cos z) = [sin(tz/2)/(t sin(z/2))]^64. Adding white-noise variance γ/(1+s) and terminal multiplier (1−γλ)Qm*(1−λ/16)/[(1+s)λ] gives exactly (1+s)^−1 times the inverse lattice Laplacian on nonconstant modes, so the bands have a checkable target spectrum.
- Demo sketch: Use Fourier sampling on a 128² periodic grid with λ(k) = 4Σi sin²(πki/128), set the constant mode to zero, and display individual bands and their sum. WITH the formulas, sample independent band modes with variances Γm and include the white and terminal terms; WITHOUT, use an arbitrary blurred octave pyramid matched at one frequency. Measure relative covariance-spectrum error, variance of low-frequency tests, and milliseconds per generated band; verify the exact multiplier sum before sampling.
- Proven vs. speculative: The finite covariance identity and positivity are proved algebraically; the manuscript credits earlier finite-range Gaussian decomposition work and supplies this explicit construction within its height/spin analysis. Terrain controls are an application proposal, and compact covariance support does not imply a compact sampling filter, a streaming generator, or a proven speedup.
- Wow: med · Confidence: high · Effort: M

## G6.5: A spin toy that exposes the cost of collective memory

- Family / papers: 227 — Universality of critical quench autocorrelations in the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model — `preprints/Universality-of-critical-quench-autocorrelations-in-the-Sherrington-Kirkpatrick-model-October-5-2026/main.pdf`; Critical mixing in the Sherrington–Kirkpatrick model — `preprints/Critical-mixing-in-the-Sherrington-Kirkpatrick-model-September-25-2026/paper.pdf`. Opened sources: the first manuscript's `build/sections/01-introduction.tex` and the second's `build/paper.tex` and `build/sections/introduction.tex`.
- Quote: "The quench starts from independent fair spins, and convergence is uniform on compact sets of positive waiting times and lags."
- Kind: visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Make an interactive array of apparently simple binary spins whose collective memory lasts much longer as the system grows at β = 1. Draw symmetric Gaussian or fair ±1 couplings Jij, compute hi = n^(−1/2)Σj≠i Jijσj, and refresh spin i to +1 with probability 1/(1+exp(−2hi)). The new results prescribe n^(2/3) per-site time and n^(1/3) mean-autocorrelation amplitude for critical comparisons, and distinguish that clock from the n^(5/3) count of attempted uniform-site updates.
- Demo sketch: Simulate n = 64, 128, 256 from independent fair spins, cache local fields, and update all affected fields after a changed spin, costing O(n) per attempt. WITH the result, compare n^(1/3)C(an^(2/3), bn^(2/3)) for fixed positive waiting-time and lag parameters a,b; WITHOUT, compare raw correlations at the same fixed sweep count. Average independent trajectories for each disorder sample and measure curve-collapse error, residual overlap, sample-to-sample spread, update count, and ms/sweep.
- Proven vs. speculative: The manuscripts prove the common Gaussian/Rademacher critical autocorrelation limit and the worst-start mixing exponent in probability over disorder, with a random limiting correlation curve that retains disorder variation. An interactive memory mechanic and visible collapse at these small sizes are speculative: no finite-n mixing certificate or practical prefactor is supplied, and dense interactions limit scale.
- Wow: med · Confidence: med · Effort: M

## G6.6: A procedural world that becomes tree-like above q = 4

- Family / papers: 211 — Brownian continuum random tree limits of finite Fortuin–Kasteleyn maps above four — `preprints/Brownian-continuum-random-tree-limits-of-finite-Fortuin-Kasteleyn-maps-above-four-September-24-2026/main.pdf`; Canonical conformal limits of subcritical FK planar maps — `preprints/Canonical-conformal-limits-of-subcritical-FK-planar-maps-September-24-2026/main.pdf`. Opened sources: the first manuscript's `build/main.tex` and `build/sections/words.tex`, and the second's `build/sections/02-model.tex` and `build/sections/03-encoding.tex`.
- Quote: "A large planar map can contain many cycles while its distances, viewed on a sufficiently large scale, approach those of a tree."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Generate planar worlds from the finite FK ensemble, with q controlling a genuine large-scale change in connectivity: the family gives quantum-surface limits for 0 < q ≤ 4 and a Brownian tree limit for fixed q > 4. An explicit existing inventory encoding uses p = sqrt(q)/(sqrt(q)+2), letters b1,b2 of probability 1/4 each, o1,o2 of probability (1−p)/4 each, and a flexible order F of probability p/2. Condition a length-2n word to empty completely, resolve each order against the freshest eligible burger, and reconstruct the spanning tree and complementary edges from the two typed matchings.
- Demo sketch: Precompute capped batches at n = 32, 64, 128 and several q values, then animate connectivity in a simple graph layout. WITH the result, sample the conditioned weighted ensemble and compare q > 4 with q < 4; WITHOUT, use a fixed q = 2 ensemble for every setting. Record rejection acceptance and generation milliseconds separately from rendering FPS, and measure diameter/sqrt(n) plus sampled four-point tree defect divided by diameter.
- Proven vs. speculative: The new theorem proves tree convergence after multiplying graph distances by c(q)n^(−1/2), while the encoding predates the manuscript; a force-directed drawing and visible small-map transition are extrapolations. No fast sampler, evaluated c(q), or rate uniform near q = 4 is supplied, and naive rejection accepts at most O(n^(−3/2)), with no usable lower bound here, so generation should remain bounded and offline.
- Wow: high · Confidence: med · Effort: L

## G6.7: An exploratory constraint-jamming toy with certified target exponents

- Family / paper: 222 — Microscopic jamming in the negative spherical perceptron — `preprints/Microscopic-jamming-in-the-negative-spherical-perceptron-September-24-2026/Microscopic-jamming-in-the-negative-spherical-perceptron-September-24-2026.pdf`. Opened source: `preprints/Microscopic-jamming-in-the-negative-spherical-perceptron-September-24-2026/build/main.tex`, including the model, threshold characterization, and numerical certificate.
- Quote: "A finite numerical certificate for these exponent intervals, together with its mathematical error bounds, is included."
- Kind: visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Show a vector y on the unit sphere constrained by M = floor(αN) Gaussian inequalities Ay ≥ −1; increasing α eventually makes the constraints infeasible. The explicit penalty is H(y) = ½Σμ max(−1−Aμy,0)², giving gaps hμ = 1+Aμy and forces normalized by the mean negative gap over violated constraints. The paper supplies limiting gap/force CDF exponents 1−γ and 1+θ, with γ = 1/(2+θ), 0.4126930 < γ < 0.4126934, and 0.4231063 < θ < 0.4231088, as concrete targets for a jamming visualization.
- Demo sketch: Use projected descent with random restarts at N = 64–256, show a two-dimensional projection of y, and plot contact-removed positive-gap and mean-one-force CDFs on logarithmic axes. WITH the result, compare fitted slopes with approximately 0.587307 and 1.423108; WITHOUT, use raw unnormalized forces or a uniform-force reference. Measure final penalty, violated fraction, slope error, restart/cooling sensitivity, and ms/optimization step.
- Proven vs. speculative: The manuscript proves equilibrium laws in the ordered limits N→∞, then inverse temperature→∞, then density approaching the threshold from above; the certificate validates exponent intervals, while the threshold itself remains a variational characterization. Projected descent has no proved equilibrium-sampling or global-convergence guarantee here, so this is a low-confidence experiment rather than a certified solver or a result about ordinary 2D/3D particle contacts.
- Wow: med · Confidence: low · Effort: L
