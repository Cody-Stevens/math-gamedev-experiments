## G8.1: A quantum momentum cloud with a proved density knob
- Family / paper: 267 — Quantum Depletion and Momentum Distribution in the Dilute Hard-Sphere Bose Gas — `preprints/Quantum-Depletion-and-Momentum-Distribution-in-the-Dilute-Hard-Sphere-Bose-Gas-October-5-2026/Quantum-Depletion-in-the-Dilute-Hard-Sphere-Bose-Gas.pdf`. Source opened: the manuscript's `build/main.tex`, `build/sections/introduction.tex`, and `build/sections/momentum.tex`.
- Quote: "The thermodynamic limit is taken at each fixed density before the dilute limit, uniformly over all pure and mixed ground states."
- Kind: visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Draw a bright zero-momentum condensate surrounded by a cloud of depleted momentum modes, with density and exclusion distance controlling the cloud's mass and scale. For gas parameter `eta = rho*a^3`, the depleted fraction is asymptotically `8/(3*sqrt(pi))*sqrt(eta)`, and momenta scale as `sqrt(8*pi*rho*a)*t`. The explicit occupation profile is `g(t) = (|t|^2+1)/(2*sqrt(|t|^4+2*|t|^2)) - 1/2`; sample directions uniformly and radii with density proportional to `r^2*g(r)`. This gives a physically grounded alternative to an arbitrarily chosen Gaussian cloud, although the Bogoliubov profile itself predates this proof.
- Demo sketch: In a small C/C++ viewer, precompute a radial cumulative-distribution table and animate momentum arrows while changing `rho` and `a` within the dilute regime. WITH the paper's formula, use its depleted fraction and momentum profile; WITHOUT, use the same particle count and characteristic momentum scale with a Gaussian radial envelope and a fitted depletion curve. Measure radial-histogram error against numerical integration of the stated profile, discarded tail mass, depletion-coefficient error, and milliseconds per update; these compare surrogate models, not an independently solved many-body ground state.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves convergence of the rescaled one-body occupation measure for exact quantum ground states in the stated iterated limits, not a finite-density error bar or a dynamical particle algorithm. Independent sampling, parameter animation, and any game mechanic built around the cloud are extrapolations; finite cutoffs and numerical sampling introduce their own errors.
- Wow: med · Confidence: high · Effort: S

## G8.2: Entanglement survives two applications of explicit PPT noise
- Family / paper: 272 — Entanglement with zero distillable secret key in local dimension ten — `preprints/Entanglement-with-zero-distillable-secret-key-in-local-dimension-ten-September-27-2026/paper.pdf`. Sources opened: `build/source/main.tex`, `build/source/sections/certificate.tex`, `build/source/sections/geometry.tex`, `build/source/sections/projection.tex`, and `build/source/sections/applications.tex`; also read the supplied `verification/exact_certificate.py` without running it.
- Quote: "The construction also disproves the unrestricted two-map PPT-composition conjecture."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization
- Idea: Build a quantum-resource puzzle in which two applications of the same noisy channel can leave entanglement intact, even though the channel has positive partial transpose (PPT). The construction starts with four explicitly listed integer `6x4` matrices and the map `L(E_ij) = M_i^T*M_j`, then uses tensor products and symmetric/exterior compressions to obtain two maps on `10x10` matrices. The paper's two-block switch and absorbing flag turn them into a trace-preserving channel `Theta` on `21x21` matrices whose square is not entanglement breaking. Unlike an existential quantum example, all matrices needed to construct this counterexample are supplied.
- Demo sketch: In C/C++, construct the maps once and feed a maximally entangled reference/system state through one and then two uses of `Theta`, displaying Choi-matrix heatmaps and the surviving entangled corner; precompute expensive eigenvalue checks. WITH the construction, retain the paper-certified nonseparable corner; WITHOUT, replace the noise by computational-basis measurement followed by preparation, an explicitly entanglement-breaking baseline. Record trace-preservation error, minimum Choi and partial-transpose eigenvalues, probability mass in the certified corner, and microseconds per channel application; PPT positivity alone is not an entanglement detector.
- Proven vs. speculative: The manuscript proves that `Theta` is trace preserving and PPT and that `Theta^2` is not entanglement breaking, using the product-free range of its compressed composite Choi matrix. The puzzle and animation are extrapolations, and floating-point diagnostics do not reproduce the exact algebraic certificate or prove entanglement by themselves.

## G8.3: Exact noisy-qubit capacity from one scalar search
- Family / paper: 276 — Classical capacity and entropy inequalities for generalized amplitude damping — `preprints/Classical-capacity-and-entropy-inequalities-for-generalized-amplitude-damping-September-24-2026/paper.pdf`. Sources opened: `build/paper.tex` and `build/sections/holevo.tex`.
- Quote: "A binary pure-state ensemble attains the one-use optimum, and its independent products attain the optimum at every block length."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | performance
- Idea: Give a quantum-communication puzzle an exact capacity budget as damping strength `gamma` and stationary excited-state population `nu` change. For `p` in `[0,1]`, set `t=(1-gamma)*p+gamma*nu`, `v=gamma*nu*(1-nu)+gamma*(1-gamma)*(p-nu)^2`, and `q=(1+sqrt(1-4*v))/2`; capacity in bits per use is `max_p [h2(t)-h2(q)]`, where `h2` is binary entropy. The attaining equiprobable signals are `sqrt(1-p)|0> +/- sqrt(p)|1>`. The new result certifies the scalar optimum against arbitrary entangled block inputs throughout the parameter range; it does not invent the previously studied one-use formula.
- Demo sketch: A C/C++ demo can draw the damped Bloch sphere and a capacity heatmap while a refined one-dimensional search updates the optimal pair of signals. WITH, display the certified scalar capacity; WITHOUT, use a fixed `p=1/2` pair or optimize only computational-basis signals, then show the missed Holevo information. Measure bits per use, the gap from the restricted baselines, and microseconds per parameter update, checking numerical convergence as the scalar search is refined.
- Proven vs. speculative: The equality is for independent memoryless channel uses, unassisted classical communication, and collective decoding; independent signal ensembles attain the Holevo optimum at every block length. Gameplay pricing and the viewer are extrapolations, and the theorem does not supply a practical collective decoder, a classical network speedup, or exact floating-point optimization.
- Wow: med · Confidence: high · Effort: S

## G8.4: Temperature-sensitive magnetism with the first lattice correction
- Family / paper: 271 — The first lattice correction to Bloch's law — `preprints/The-first-lattice-correction-to-Blochs-law-October-5-2026/first-lattice-correction-bloch-law.pdf`. Supplement: Bloch's Law for Finite-Range Heisenberg Ferromagnets in Three Dimensions — `preprints/Blochs-Law-for-Finite-Range-Heisenberg-Ferromagnets-in-Three-Dimensions-October-5-2026/bloch-law-heisenberg.pdf`. Opened both manuscripts' `build/sections/01-introduction.tex`, and the correction manuscript's `build/main.tex`.
- Quote: "The magnetization is the right derivative at zero field of the thermodynamic pressure; the volume limit precedes the field derivative, and the low-temperature limit is taken last."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Use temperature to change the strength of a quantum magnetic material or a magnetism-themed game resource, rather than reducing its strength by an arbitrary linear rule. For unit nearest-neighbor coupling, spin `S`, and `T=1/beta`, the deficit is `S-m = zeta(3/2)/(8*pi^(3/2))*(T/S)^(3/2) + 3*zeta(5/2)/(128*pi^(3/2))*(T/S)^(5/2) + o((T/S)^(5/2))`. The companion supplies an anisotropic leading coefficient: with `D=(S/2)*sum_z J(z)*z*z^T`, the leading deficit is `zeta(3/2)*T^(3/2)/(8*pi^(3/2)*sqrt(det(D)))` for the stated nonnegative finite-range lattice-generating couplings. These are newly justified equilibrium laws for the interacting quantum model, rather than newly discovered spin-wave formulas.
- Demo sketch: Render lattice arrows and a material-strength gauge in C/C++, sweeping low temperature with fixed `S`. WITH, use the two-term nearest-neighbor law; WITHOUT, use only the leading Bloch term, comparing both against numerical quadrature of the full ideal-magnon density with dispersion `epsilon(k)=2*S*sum_j(1-cos(k_j))`. Measure absolute and relative magnetization-deficit error, the error's temperature scaling, and evaluation time; the quadrature is an asymptotic reference, not an exact finite quantum-spin simulation.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves agreement with the full ideal-magnon density through error `o(beta^(-5/2))` in the thermodynamic-first, zero-field, low-temperature limit, with fixed quantum spin. Finite visual lattices and force/strength gameplay are surrogates; no computable safe-temperature threshold, finite-volume accuracy guarantee, classical-spin dynamics theorem, or phase-transition location is supplied.
- Wow: med · Confidence: high · Effort: S

## G8.5: A six-symbol puzzle with exactly three neutral measurement modes
- Family / paper: 266 — The maximum number of mutually unbiased bases in dimension six — `preprints/The-maximum-number-of-mutually-unbiased-bases-in-dimension-six-September-24-2026/The-maximum-number-of-mutually-unbiased-bases-in-dimension-six-September-24-2026.pdf`. Sources opened: `build/sections/introduction.tex` and `build/sections/seed.tex`.
- Quote: "The upper bound is computer-assisted: under the stated binary64 arithmetic and compiler conditions, a complete execution of the documented verification pipeline excludes four arbitrary complex bases."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Store a six-component complex state and let players inspect it through three measurement modes: after an outcome in one mode, repeating that mode is deterministic, while switching to either other mode gives all six outcomes equal probability. The supplied construction is `U_r=A_r tensor B_r`: `A_0=I2`, `A_1=[[1,1],[1,-1]]/sqrt(2)`, `A_2=[[1,1],[i,-i]]/sqrt(2)`; `B_0=I3`, `B_1=F3`, `B_2=diag(1,omega,omega)*F3`, with `omega=exp(2*pi*i/3)` and `(F3)_jk=omega^(j*k)/sqrt(3)`. Compute outcome probabilities as `|(U_r^* psi)_j|^2` and collapse to the selected column. The new upper bound makes three an exact design ceiling for these perfectly neutral orthonormal measurement modes in dimension six; the triple construction itself is older.
- Demo sketch: A small C/C++ puzzle viewer can display the state amplitudes and the three `6x6` transition-probability heatmaps. WITH, use the exact tensor-product bases; WITHOUT, use three generic unitary bases and show their unequal cross-mode probabilities. Measure maximum deviation from `1/6`, orthogonality error, empirical outcome frequencies, and microseconds per measurement; a numerical attempt at a fourth mode illustrates the obstruction but does not prove it.
- Proven vs. speculative: Under the paper's stated computational arithmetic/compiler contract, the manuscript establishes `N(6)=3` and gives an explicit attaining triple. The mechanic is an extrapolation, uniformity applies after collapse to a basis vector rather than to every arbitrary input state, and the paper does not give a quantitative best-approximate-fourth-mode bound.
- Wow: med · Confidence: med · Effort: S

## G8.6: A classical optimizer for random, conflicting faction affinities
- Family / paper: 281 — Full support of the zero-temperature Sherrington-Kirkpatrick order parameter — `preprints/Full-support-of-the-zero-temperature-Sherrington-Kirkpatrick-order-parameter-September-27-2026/main.pdf`. Sources opened: `build/main.tex`, `build/consequences.tex`, and `build/algorithm-application.tex`. This lead comes from the companion, not from the family's QAOA circuit theorem.
- Quote: "Thus its support has no gaps at any overlap scale below one."
- Kind: gameplay | performance | visualization
- Idea: Assign actors to two factions with signs `sigma_i` in `{-1,1}` and independent Gaussian pair affinities `J_ij`, maximizing `H_n(sigma)=sum_(i<j) J_ij*sigma_i*sigma_j/sqrt(n)`. The full-support theorem verifies the missing no-overlap-gap hypothesis of an existing classical incremental approximate-message-passing optimizer. The paper's algorithmic corollary then gives, for each fixed `epsilon>0`, at most `C(epsilon)*n^2` real-arithmetic operations and probability tending to one of returning a score within `epsilon*n` of the optimum. This is a new guarantee for an existing algorithm, with a plausible bridge to a frustrated-faction puzzle rather than a quantum-hardware speed claim.
- Demo sketch: Obtain the cited finite-disorder IAMP recurrence and precompute its Parisi scalar tables before implementing a C/C++ viewer for a few hundred actors; this is a larger project, not a turnkey few-hundred-line algorithm furnished by the manuscript. WITH, run that optimizer; WITHOUT, run random-restart greedy spin flips and simulated annealing on identical Gaussian instances with equal time budgets, displaying factions and score traces. Measure energy per actor, time to reach a target score, milliseconds per dense matrix-vector step, and memory, using exhaustive search at small sizes to measure actual optimality gaps.
- Proven vs. speculative: The asymptotic guarantee concerns the pure zero-field Gaussian SK model, assumes prescribed scalar functions and unit-cost real arithmetic, and gives no finite-size rate, preprocessing complexity, finite-precision guarantee, or numerical bound on `C(epsilon)`. Practical advantage at the proposed demo sizes, arbitrary affinity graphs, sparse worlds, external biases, and balanced-team constraints are unproved extrapolations; the scalar Gaussian construction in `consequences.tex` is explicitly not the finite-instance optimizer.
- Wow: med · Confidence: med · Effort: L
