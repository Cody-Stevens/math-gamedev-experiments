## G1.1: A cheap proxy for prime-factor fragmentation

- Family / paper: 011 — The Poisson-Dirichlet law for prime predecessors — `preprints/The-Poisson-Dirichlet-Law-for-Prime-Predecessors-September-24-2026/paper.pdf`. TeX checked: `preprints/The-Poisson-Dirichlet-Law-for-Prime-Predecessors-September-24-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex` and `preprints/The-Poisson-Dirichlet-Law-for-Prime-Predecessors-September-24-2026/build/sections/08-poisson-dirichlet.tex`.
- Quote: "We prove that the same limiting partition occurs for the predecessor of a uniformly chosen prime."
- Kind: visualization | performance | gameplay
- Idea: Turn the prime factors of a uniformly sampled prime predecessor into fragment sizes: list the factors q_j of p-1 with multiplicity and assign mass log(q_j)/log(p-1). The manuscript identifies their ranked limiting distribution as PD(1), so a simulation needing these aggregate statistics could use stick breaking instead of arithmetic factorization. Generate independent uniform U_j in (0,1), form B_j=(1-U_j) times the product of the earlier U_i, then sort the fragments; render resource shares, broken plates, or a strip of ranked pieces. This supplies a statistical proxy for this particular arithmetic partition, without recovering actual primes or exact factorizations.
- Demo sketch: WITHOUT the proxy, sieve a prime table, sample its primes uniformly, and factor p-1 to draw its exact logarithmic partition; WITH it, draw and sort 16 or 24 stick fragments, displaying the undrawn mass separately. Compare largest-piece CDFs and the first-two-piece joint histogram as the prime cutoff grows, and measure microseconds per partition and memory. Report prime-table setup and both direct factorization and an amortized smallest-prime-factor-table baseline, so any speedup is measured fairly.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves convergence of every fixed finite collection of ranked coordinates and gives the explicit stick representation; its expected residual after d draws is 2^-d, and each ranked-coordinate truncation error is at most that residual. Stick breaking is an existing sampler whose newly claimed applicability is to prime predecessors: finite-cutoff accuracy, a practical speedup, and a game fragmentation model remain speculative, and 2^-d does not bound the discrepancy from finite primes or justify independent recursive fragmentation.
- Wow: med · Confidence: med · Effort: S

## G1.2: A threshold for rare resonance bursts

- Family / paper: 017 — The irrationality exponent of pi is 2 — `preprints/The-irrationality-exponent-of-pi-is-2-September-24-2026/paper.pdf`. TeX checked: `preprints/The-irrationality-exponent-of-pi-is-2-September-24-2026/build/main.tex` and `preprints/The-irrationality-exponent-of-pi-is-2-September-24-2026/build/sections/series.tex`.
- Quote: "The classical Flint--Hills series converges."
- Kind: sim-design-rule | visualization
- Idea: At integer tick n, rotate an emitter to angle n radians and emit a positive impulse w_n=1/(n^a |sin n|^b), with a,b>0. Near alignment, the small sine produces rare large bursts, while a controls decay and b controls resonance strength. The generalized Flint-Hills corollary gives a sharp lifetime-budget rule: the sum of impulses is finite exactly when a>max{1,b}. Use that boundary as a knob for a scalar dose, cumulative brightness, or an additive velocity toy.
- Demo sketch: WITH the rule, use a=3,b=2; WITHOUT it, choose a=2,b=2 on the assumption that inverse-square temporal decay suffices. Draw the rotating emitter, burst heights, and cumulative dose, with parameter sliders and the theoretical boundary; measure total dose S_N, dose added in successive dyadic blocks, maximum block impulse, and milliseconds per million ticks. This compares parameter choices informed by the theorem, and a finite recording should show the observed bursts without claiming to prove convergence or divergence.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves the summability criterion for this exact integer-radian scalar sequence, including divergence on the boundary; the emitter and game interpretation are extrapolations. It supplies no effective tail-error bound or certified settling time, and the result does not establish numerical-integrator stability, stability at another angular frequency, or protection from individual large early impulses; floating-point sine accuracy also needs separate checking at large n.
- Wow: med · Confidence: med · Effort: S

## G1.3: Cubic interference with a persistent hidden bias

- Family / paper: 023 — An unconditional first moment for cubic Gauss sums — `preprints/An-unconditional-first-moment-for-cubic-Gauss-sums-September-25-2026/paper.pdf`. TeX checked: `preprints/An-unconditional-first-moment-for-cubic-Gauss-sums-September-25-2026/build/paper.tex` and `preprints/An-unconditional-first-moment-for-cubic-Gauss-sums-September-25-2026/build/sections/angular.tex`.
- Quote: "We prove Patterson's first-moment conjecture for normalized cubic Gauss sums over all primary Eisenstein primes, unconditionally."
- Kind: visualization | gameplay
- Idea: For each rational prime p congruent to 1 modulo 3, draw unit arrows with phases 2π(x^3 mod p)/p for x=0,...,p-1 and add them head-to-tail. Their sum S_p looks irregular, but the paper predicts a positive cumulative first moment M(X)=sum_{p≤X, p≡1 mod 3} S_p/(2√p) asymptotic to K X^(5/6)/log X, where K=(2π)^(2/3)/(5Γ(2/3)). Animate the interference walk and use its endpoint as a puzzle clue or an example of structured phases whose aggregate behavior differs from independent randomness. The new contribution is the claimed unconditional bias law, rather than the already computable cubic phases.
- Demo sketch: WITH the construction, use modular cubic phases and display their interference walks and running M(X); WITHOUT it, use p independent uniform phases with the same normalization and renderer. Overlay the explicit prediction and a variance band from repeated random baselines, measuring M(X), its ratio to K X^(5/6)/log X, imaginary residual, milliseconds per prime, and render fps. A small sieve and O(p) direct sums suffice at modest cutoffs; compute the sums as bounded setup work and reuse them during animation.
- Proven vs. speculative: The manuscript proves the stated rational-prime consequence via its pairing identity; its all-primary-Eisenstein-prime theorem instead has coefficient (6/5)c_*, with c_*=(2π)^(2/3)/(3Γ(2/3)), so those normalizations must not be mixed. It gives little-oh error without a usable finite-cutoff accuracy guarantee or faster sum algorithm; visibility of the bias at demo scale and gameplay value remain experimental, and the source explicitly discusses conflicting historical coefficient conventions.
- Wow: high · Confidence: med · Effort: M

## G1.4: Periodic arithmetic islands with a connectivity certificate

- Family / paper: 028 — Bounded-Step Walks on Gaussian Primes — `preprints/Bounded-Step-Walks-on-Gaussian-Primes-September-26-2026/paper.pdf`. TeX checked: `preprints/Bounded-Step-Walks-on-Gaussian-Primes-September-26-2026/build/main.tex` and `preprints/Bounded-Step-Walks-on-Gaussian-Primes-September-26-2026/build/periodicity.tex`.
- Quote: "The proof constructs a finite periodic sieve obstruction using geometric sampling and information-theoretic estimates."
- Kind: visualization | gameplay | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Generate a periodic lattice terrain by keeping (x,y) traversable exactly when (x²+y²) mod p is nonzero for every selected prime p congruent to 1 modulo 4. For step radius D, a BFS on the finite repeating tile can assign unwrapped coordinates; a conflicting assignment certifies a cycle with nonzero translation and hence an infinite lifted component, while no conflict certifies finite islands. The manuscript proves that a finite prime set producing such an obstruction exists for every finite D and uses it to bound every Gaussian-prime component. A movement puzzle could expose how one extra arithmetic restriction closes apparently endless corridors.
- Demo sketch: WITH the obstruction, use P={5,13}, D=1 and a 65×65 tile; WITHOUT the extra restriction, use P={5}, D=1 and a 5×5 tile. An independent in-memory exhaustive check during this scan found 2,304 traversable residues, 243 components, largest component 580, and no winding component for the two-prime mask; the one-prime mask has a winding component and therefore an infinite corridor. Color components on a scrolling repeated world and measure BFS milliseconds, reachable size, and winding-component count; the masks have different densities, so label this as an arithmetic-factor ablation.
- Proven vs. speculative: For a verified prime set with Q=product(P), the paper gives a Q² bound for sieve-mask components and the Gaussian-prime bound max{Q², |E|+(K_D-1)|E|Q²}, where E consists of associates of the selected Gaussian factors and K_D counts lattice increments of length at most D, including zero. The general prime set and component bound are nonexplicit, and small-radius obstructions predate this result: the checked toy illustrates the proof mechanism, while practical obstructions at larger movement radii and a game benefit from the new general theorem remain unestablished.
- Wow: med · Confidence: med · Effort: S

## G1.5: Arithmetic textures with fixed-shift decorrelation

- Family / paper: 007 — Ordinary two-point correlations of multiplicative functions — `preprints/Ordinary-two-point-correlations-of-multiplicative-functions-September-24-2026/final.pdf`. TeX checked: `preprints/Ordinary-two-point-correlations-of-multiplicative-functions-September-24-2026/build/introduction.tex`; the quoted sentence is from the manuscript abstract in CONTENTS.md.
- Quote: "For every fixed pair of nonproportional affine forms, the Liouville correlation has a power-of-logarithm saving at every cutoff, with an absolute exponent."
- Kind: visualization | gameplay
- Idea: Compute λ(n)=(-1)^Ω(n), where Ω counts prime factors with multiplicity, and use its signs as a repeatable binary texture or a one-dimensional content stream. The theorem bounds the ordinary correlation of two fixed nonproportional affine-indexed channels by C X/(log X)^c, with absolute c>0. Display local unpredictability alongside exact arithmetic structure, such as λ(2n)=-λ(n), to make an arithmetic-noise puzzle or texture inspector. The new claim is a decorrelation theorem for an existing sequence, rather than a new random-number generator.
- Demo sketch: WITH the arithmetic sequence, build a smallest-prime-factor table and compute λ(n)=-λ(n/spf(n)); WITHOUT it, render a standard seeded PRNG binary texture using the same display and sample count. Show fixed-offset correlations, run-length histograms, and the doubling anti-correlation, measuring preparation milliseconds, lookup time, memory, and correlation magnitude. Keep the set of tested affine forms fixed as the cutoff grows, matching the theorem's quantifiers.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves the fixed-form bound but says its constants need not be effective and asserts no uniformity for coefficients growing with X. A usable finite-size quality threshold, higher-order independence, suitability as general two-dimensional coordinate noise, and any advantage over the PRNG are unproved; this is a low-confidence procedural-content lead with no claimed algorithmic speedup.
- Wow: low · Confidence: low · Effort: S
