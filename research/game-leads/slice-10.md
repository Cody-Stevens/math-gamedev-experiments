## G10.1: A periodic blob switches from ball to tube to slab

- Family / paper: 354 — The Isoperimetric Conjecture for the Cubic Flat Three-Torus — `preprints/The-Isoperimetric-Conjecture-for-the-Cubic-Flat-Three-Torus-September-24-2026/build/paper.tex` (abstract and main theorem).
- Quote: "The minimizing regions are balls, circular tubes about shortest closed geodesics, coordinate slabs, and their complements."
- Kind: visualization | performance | sim-design-rule
- Idea: A minimum-area blob in a unit periodic cube has an exact shape at every occupied volume, giving both a procedural object and a reference solution for surface relaxation. With $v=\min(V,1-V)$, its minimum area is $I(V)=\min\{(36\pi)^{1/3}v^{2/3},\,2\sqrt{\pi v},\,2\}$. Below half volume, use a ball of radius $(3V/4\pi)^{1/3}$ up to $4\pi/81$, a wrapping circular tube of radius $\sqrt{V/\pi}$ up to $1/\pi$, then a coordinate slab of width $V$; above half volume, take complements. At each transition, both adjacent types minimize, which gives a visible topology change with an exact threshold.
- Demo sketch: Build a C/C++ periodic implicit-surface renderer with a volume slider and show both co-minimizers at each transition. WITH: generate the classified shape directly; WITHOUT: relax voxel occupancy by diffusion and thresholding at a fixed occupied count, then compare its output to the analytic target. Measure area excess $A/I(V)-1$, volume error, generation time, and relaxation steps; any speed advantage concerns producing a global minimizer, rather than simulating the physical transition.
- Proven vs. speculative: The manuscript classifies all finite-perimeter global minimizers in the cubic flat three-torus, including transition equality cases. Procedural generation and solver benchmarking follow directly, but convergence of local relaxation, transition dynamics, rectangular boxes, and extra physical energies are outside the theorem.
- Wow: high · Confidence: high · Effort: S

## G10.2: Fluid gates that move whole regions without compressing them

- Family / paper: 376 — Finite Instructions and Solenoidal Shear Flows — `preprints/Finite-Instructions-and-Solenoidal-Shear-Flows-September-27-2026/build/main.tex` (abstract, initialized-computation theorem, seven-stage construction, and force formula).
- Quote: "We realize finite reciprocal affine instruction maps by smooth incompressible shear flows on a flat three-torus."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Turn a finite rule table into fluid gates: lift each source rectangle to its own height, reshape it with four shears, translate it, then lower it into its destination. For $X(a)(r,s)=(r+as,s)$ and $Y(a)(r,s)=(r,s+ar)$, the chronological sequence $Y(-\lambda),X(\lambda^{-1}-1),Y(1),X(\lambda-1)$ gives $(r,s)\mapsto(\lambda r,s/\lambda)$. If source and destination coordinate half-widths are at most $h$, every intermediate scaling trajectory stays within $2h$ of the source center, independently of $\lambda$. The paper supplies smooth cutoff and pulse formulas, and its velocity has zero divergence, zero mean, and zero self-advection, so the exact Navier–Stokes force is simply $f=V_t-\nu\Delta V$ at any fixed positive computable viscosity.
- Demo sketch: In a small C/C++ particle display, implement two or three overlapping source/destination gates with moderate rational scale factors and show their separate routing heights. WITH: use the seven stages and sampled prescribed velocity; WITHOUT: interpolate each rectangle directly between its endpoints, which generally changes area during the move and can interfere with other branches. Measure particle endpoint error, enclosed-area or volume drift, maximum excursion, and milliseconds per update; a finite reversible rule table can make the gates into a visual logic puzzle.
- Proven vs. speculative: The construction acts exactly on neighborhoods of whole rectangles and compiles arbitrary machines into forced flows with an exact particle halting detector. A finite gate demo is constructive and bounded, but the unbounded computation uses exact real coordinates, has no finite-precision robustness guarantee, and is not a practical game-loop computer; sampling the prescribed field also does not validate a numerical Navier–Stokes solver.
- Wow: high · Confidence: high · Effort: M

## G10.3: A tiny moving hub can reassign a much larger crowd

- Family / paper: 374 — Sharp One-Third Stability of Brenier Maps — `preprints/Sharp-One-Third-Stability-of-Brenier-Maps-September-25-2026/build/source/main.tex`, `preprints/Sharp-One-Third-Stability-of-Brenier-Maps-September-25-2026/build/source/sections/sharpness.tex`, and `preprints/Sharp-One-Third-Stability-of-Brenier-Maps-September-25-2026/build/source/sections/gradient.tex` (abstract, explicit three-atom construction, and admissible stability constant).
- Quote: "A three-atom family on a fixed cube disproves Letrouit's conjectured uniform square-root estimate."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Use the explicit counterexample as an allocation toy in which a small hub displacement makes a visible strip of agents switch destinations. For uniform agents on $[-1,1]^2$, $0<a<1/2$, and $b=a/2$, assign them by the gradients of $u_a=\max\{-x_1,x_1,a\}$ and $\widetilde u_a=\max\{-x_1,x_1,a+bx_2\}$. Both destination measures put mass $(1-a)/2$ at each of $\pm e_1$ and mass $a$ at their central hub, which moves from $0$ to $be_2$; the exact target distance is $W_2=a^{3/2}/2$, while assignment-map RMS change is $\sqrt a\sqrt{1+a^2}/2$. These formulas expose the sharp cube-root sensitivity of optimal transport, relevant when a crowd allocator or morphing effect treats target motion as a proxy for agent motion.
- Demo sketch: Draw the two central cells $|x_1|<a$ and $|x_1|<a+bx_2$ and animate the reassigned agents as $a$ decreases. WITH: recompute the explicit optimal cells; WITHOUT: keep old labels and move only the central hub, preserving the target masses but losing optimality. Measure RMS map change, changed-label fraction $a/4$, transport-cost excess of frozen labels, and the log–log slope against $W_2$, which tends to $1/3$ as $a$ tends to zero.
- Proven vs. speculative: The manuscript proves the explicit identities and a uniform $L^2$ cube-root bound for quadratic optimal maps from a uniform convex-body source to targets in a fixed compact set, with an optimal exponent and an explicit admissible geometric constant. Crowd allocation and morphing are applications; the bound controls aggregate RMS change and does not guarantee pointwise stability for each agent.
- Wow: high · Confidence: high · Effort: S

## G10.4: A positive shrinking geometry with exploding curvature

- Family / paper: 352 — A finite-time singularity of Calabi flow on projective space — `preprints/A-finite-time-singularity-of-Calabi-flow-on-projective-space-September-24-2026/build/main.tex`, `preprints/A-finite-time-singularity-of-Calabi-flow-on-projective-space-September-24-2026/build/sections/profile.tex`, `preprints/A-finite-time-singularity-of-Calabi-flow-on-projective-space-September-24-2026/build/sections/geometry.tex`, and `preprints/A-finite-time-singularity-of-Calabi-flow-on-projective-space-September-24-2026/build/sections/compact.tex`.
- Quote: "This disproves Chen's smooth long-time existence conjecture, even in a class containing a constant-scalar-curvature metric."
- Kind: visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: The compact counterexample has complex dimension ten, but its explicit shrinking core is a scalar radial profile that needs no high-dimensional grid. Put $\rho=\sqrt{-t}$, $y=x/\rho$, $c=(1+d)^{-1}$, $r=\sqrt{27/16}\,y/c$, and $G=c(1+p)$, where $p=d+kr+\sum_{i\ge2}a_i r^i$ and $a_i=[r^{i-2}]((1+p)^{-1}-1)/((27/8)(i-1)(i+9)(i+10))$. The paper confines the matching parameters to $|k+0.094617116666041|\le4.44605\times10^{-13}$ and $|d-0.400682721856639|\le5.40506\times10^{-12}$, and proves convergence for $|r|<3$. Then $F(t,x)=\rho^{-1}(G(y)-1)/y$ solves the flat model $F_t+(1+xF)^2(x\partial_x^2+12\partial_x)^2F=0$, with positive multiplier $1+xF=G(y)$ and center scalar curvature $-110G'(0)/\rho$ growing like $(-t)^{-1/2}$.
- Demo sketch: Precompute the recurrence, evaluate a truncated series on $0\le r\le1$ using the central parameter values, and animate the shrinking core with curvature colors and a plot of its center value. WITH: the constructed profile; WITHOUT: the stationary flat profile $F=0$, with the compact equilibrium $H=0$ optionally shown in a separately labeled panel. Measure truncation sensitivity, flat-equation residual, minimum positivity multiplier, frame time, and the predicted curvature exponent $-1/2$; a circular rendering illustrates radial data rather than an ordinary two-dimensional surface flow.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves the flat profile and an exact compact singular flow on $\mathbb{CP}^{10}$, but obtains the compact correction $H=F+V$ through existence arguments. The small demo reproduces an approximation to the explicit flat core; the parameter midpoint is not asserted to be the exact globally matched profile, and neither the compact counterexample nor numerical stability is reproduced by that animation.
- Wow: med · Confidence: med · Effort: M

## G10.5: Correct a dilute gas's particle count at the noise scale

- Family / paper: 364 — Hard-sphere fluctuations on the regular Boltzmann lifespan — `preprints/Hard-sphere-fluctuations-on-the-regular-Boltzmann-lifespan-September-23-2026/build/main.tex`, `preprints/Hard-sphere-fluctuations-on-the-regular-Boltzmann-lifespan-September-23-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex`, and `preprints/Hard-sphere-fluctuations-on-the-regular-Boltzmann-lifespan-September-23-2026/build/sections/02-initial-target.tex` (ensemble law, initial fluctuation theorem, and first-order mean expansion).
- Quote: "Thus exclusion changes the mean at exactly the fluctuation scale, even though its contribution to the limiting initial covariance vanishes."
- Kind: visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: A dilute hard-sphere cloud can look correctly initialized while its expected count is wrong by an entire sampling-noise scale. For the paper's pair-excluded grand-canonical law with sphere diameter $\epsilon$, activity $\mu=\epsilon^{-2}$, and smooth spatial probability density $\varrho_0$, integrating its initial mean expansion gives $(\mathbb EN-\mu)/\sqrt\mu\to-b$, where $b=(4\pi/3)\int\varrho_0^2$, while $\operatorname{Var}(N)/\mu\to1$. Thus the leading corrected center is $\mu-b\sqrt\mu$; a spatial Gaussian of standard deviation $\sigma$ gives the explicit value $b=1/(6\sqrt\pi\,\sigma^3)$. The correction remains visible in normalized count histograms even as excluded volume disappears in the dilute limit.
- Demo sketch: Sample the specified law in C/C++ using equal-probability birth/death Metropolis proposals and a spatial hash: propose births from $\varrho_0$, reject overlaps, otherwise accept with $\min(1,\mu/(N+1))$; delete a uniformly selected particle with acceptance $\min(1,N/\mu)$. WITH: center the resulting counts at $\mu-b\sqrt\mu$; WITHOUT: center the same samples at $\mu$, and overlay their normalized histograms as $\epsilon$ decreases. Measure normalized mean bias, variance, Gaussian-fit error, and effective independent samples per second after equilibration; no collision evolution or kinetic PDE is needed.
- Proven vs. speculative: The mean correction follows from the manuscript's initial expansion, and its bounded-test fluctuation theorem supplies the count's Gaussian limit for the stated smooth ensemble. The finite-$\epsilon$ correction and sampler are proposed implementations requiring convergence and mixing checks; this is not a correction for fixed-$N$ initialization, arbitrary sequential placement, or a proven faster sampling algorithm.
- Wow: med · Confidence: high · Effort: M

## G10.6: A spectral steering field with no interior dead ends

- Family / paper: 369 — Strict hot spots and absence of interior critical points on smooth simply connected planar domains — `preprints/Strict-hot-spots-and-absence-of-interior-critical-points-on-smooth-simply-connected-planar-domains-September-24-2026/build/main.tex` and `preprints/Strict-hot-spots-and-absence-of-interior-critical-points-on-smooth-simply-connected-planar-domains-September-24-2026/build/sections/01-introduction.tex` (abstract and main theorem).
- Quote: "More precisely, every nonzero eigenfunction for the first positive Neumann eigenvalue has nonvanishing gradient in the interior, so all its global maxima and minima lie on the boundary."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: In a smooth bounded simply connected planar room, use the gradient of a first-positive Neumann eigenfunction as a direction field for a heat-following crowd or a navigation puzzle. The new guarantee excludes every interior zero of the gradient, including saddles, without requiring symmetry or convexity. Along continuum gradient ascent, $du/dt=|\nabla u|^2>0$, so an interior equilibrium cannot trap an agent; extrema and possible stalls lie on the boundary. This provides a topology-sensitive design rule rather than routing to a chosen arbitrary goal.
- Demo sketch: Approximate a smooth nonconvex room without holes on a small grid and obtain its first mode by repeated Neumann heat steps, subtracting the mean and normalizing from a seed with a nonzero first-mode component. WITH: steer agents using that isolated mode; WITHOUT: use an arbitrary mixture of low modes or a finite-time noisy heat field, then compare interior stalls and streamlines. Measure eigen-residual, gradient zeros or near-stalls on a fixed interior region, sensitivity to grid refinement, and milliseconds per update; show boundary equilibria separately.
- Proven vs. speculative: The theorem applies to every nonzero member of the first-positive eigenspace on a smooth simply connected planar domain, including multiplicity. Numerical eigenmodes and steering are extrapolations: the paper gives no uniform positive gradient lower bound, finite-grid guarantee, boundary-arrival time, or applicability to rooms containing holes.
- Wow: med · Confidence: med · Effort: M

## G10.7: Volumetric interpolation that controls the steepest slope

- Family / paper: 377 — Uniform Interior $C^{1,\alpha}$ Estimates for Infinity-Harmonic Functions — `preprints/Uniform-Interior-C1alpha-Estimates-for-Infinity-Harmonic-Functions-October-4-2026/build/manuscript/sections/01-introduction.tex` (uniform estimate, absolute-minimization interpretation, and limitations).
- Quote: "The exponent arises from a compactness argument and is not explicit."
- Kind: visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Use an infinity-harmonic scalar field to interpolate designer-specified boundary values of a three-dimensional density, temperature, or other volumetric control field while minimizing the largest local slope. The equation is $\Delta_\infty u=\langle D^2u\,\nabla u,\nabla u\rangle=0$; the new result guarantees that the gradient is locally continuous with a positive Hölder exponent in dimension three and higher, rather than merely asserting differentiability at each point. On $B_{1/2}$ its gradient supremum and Hölder seminorm are bounded by $C_d\operatorname{osc}_{B_1}u$. This supports smooth interior gradient-driven effects for a maximum-slope objective, although absolute minimization and numerical solvers predate this result.
- Demo sketch: For matched boundary data on a small 3D voxel volume, compare an experimental infinity-Laplace solve to an ordinary harmonic solve, rendering scalar slices and gradient streamlines. WITH: target the maximum-slope objective; WITHOUT: minimize average squared gradient through harmonic interpolation. Measure maximum estimated slope, interior gradient variation under grid refinement, nonlinear residual, and milliseconds per sweep; the theorem's contribution is the continuum gradient-regularity claim, not a new solver or predicted speedup.
- Proven vs. speculative: The manuscript proves a uniform interior $C^{1,\alpha_d}$ estimate for bounded continuum infinity-harmonic functions, with a nonexplicit exponent and no endpoint or boundary claim. The volumetric application and discretization are speculative, and the existence of $C_d,\alpha_d$ does not provide numerical tolerances, finite-resolution convergence, nonvanishing gradients, or a practical stability bound.
- Wow: low · Confidence: med · Effort: M
