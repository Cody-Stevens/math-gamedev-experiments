## G7.1: A precise noise knob for hidden-faction clues

- Family / paper: 229 — The exact reconstruction threshold for the three-state symmetric channel — `preprints/The-exact-reconstruction-threshold-for-the-three-state-symmetric-channel-September-25-2026/paper.pdf`
- Quote: "At every admissible equality $d\lambda^2=1$, there is non-reconstruction."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Hide one of three factions at the root of a branching rumor network and reveal only the deepest descendants. The explicit channel keeps the parent's faction with probability `(1+2*lambda)/3` and changes to each other faction with probability `(1-lambda)/3`, for `-1/2 <= lambda <= 1`. The new classification says distant clues retain information exactly when `d*lambda*lambda > 1`, including both reinforcing and contrary rumors; equality loses information in the infinite-depth limit. This gives a mathematically calibrated boundary between recoverable and unrecoverable clues on regular trees, rather than guessing difficulty from per-edge noise alone.
- Demo sketch: In C/C++, generate finite `d`-ary trees, initialize observed leaves with one-hot faction probabilities, and propagate exact messages upward using `p_parent(i) proportional to product_children((1-lambda)/3 + lambda*p_child(i))`. WITH the theorem and recursion, place settings on either side of the threshold and display the inferred root; WITHOUT, use a leaf-majority estimator and a difficulty rule based only on the edge error rate. Over many trials and increasing depths, record mean posterior total-variation advantage over uniform, root accuracy above `1/3`, and milliseconds per inference.
- Proven vs. speculative: The manuscript proves the infinite-depth threshold for every regular branching degree at least two and observed Poisson trees of mean greater than one, and supplies the exact finite-tree posterior recursion; source checked in `build/sections/01-introduction.tex` and `02-foundations.tex`. The puzzle and its finite-depth difficulty are extrapolations: the threshold supplies no uniform practical depth at which the asymptotic contrast must become visible, and this tree recursion is not a guarantee for arbitrary graphs with loops.
- Wow: high · Confidence: high · Effort: S

## G7.2: Wave traps that spend their binding budget optimally

- Family / paper: 262 — Equality cases in the sharp one-dimensional matrix Lieb–Thirring inequality — `preprints/Equality-cases-in-the-sharp-one-dimensional-matrix-Lieb-Thirring-inequality-October-5-2026/sharp-one-dimensional-lieb-thirring-inequalities-matrix-potentials.pdf`
- Quote: "The nonzero solitons may have independent scales and centers."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Make a one-dimensional wave-trap puzzle with two internal channels, where players move and resize attractive wells under a fixed potential budget. At exponent `gamma=1`, the operator `H=-d²/dx²-W` obeys `sum_negative |E_j| <= C1*integral tr(W^(3/2)) dx`, with `C1=4/(3*sqrt(3)*pi)`, independent of channel count. Every optimizer is a constant unitary rotation of independent profiles `w_j(x)=3*a_j²*sech²(2*a_j*(x-x_j))`, each with exactly one bound-state energy `-a_j²`. The new matrix theorem and complete equality classification identify the best achievable binding and all shapes that attain it; the scalar soliton profile itself is older mathematics.
- Demo sketch: WITH the result, render these wells and analytic stationary densities proportional to `sech(2*a_j*(x-x_j))`, with exact energy labels. WITHOUT, rotate the same unequal channel profiles by a position-dependent unitary matrix, preserving the trace-power budget pointwise, and compute the spectrum on a finite grid. Record the binding-efficiency ratio against the sharp budget, eigenvalue error under box and grid refinement, and solve time when controls change; rendering needs no eigensolve every frame.
- Proven vs. speculative: The sharp bound and optimizer classification hold on the real line for measurable Hermitian positive-semidefinite finite-matrix potentials with integrable trace power and `1/2 < gamma < 3/2`; source checked in `build/sections/introduction.tex` and `rigidity.tex`. The puzzle and finite-grid solver are extrapolations, with truncation and discretization errors to measure; the theorem supplies neither a general fast eigensolver nor a time-integration stability guarantee.
- Wow: med · Confidence: high · Effort: M

## G7.3: Rough terrain with a hard slope limit and Gaussian large-scale statistics

- Family / paper: 232 — Gaussian free-field limits of weighted integer Lipschitz heights — `preprints/Gaussian-free-field-limits-of-weighted-integer-Lipschitz-heights-September-25-2026/paper.pdf`
- Quote: "We prove a Gaussian free field scaling limit for weighted integer Lipschitz heights on the triangular lattice."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Generate integer-height terrain on a triangular lattice with zero boundary heights and neighboring differences at most one. Weight each changed edge by `x`, with `1/sqrt(2) <= x <= 1`, to tune local terrace preferences while retaining a Gaussian free field limit after a model-dependent normalization. Its contours are explicit nonintersecting honeycomb loops, with exact weight `2^(number of loops)*x^(occupied edges)`, so the same sample gives both a terrain mesh and nested outlines. The new result certifies large-scale field statistics for this existing hard-slope model; it does not introduce a faster terrain generator.
- Demo sketch: Implement single-site heat-bath updates: choose an integer `k` in `[max_neighbor(h-1), min_neighbor(h+1)]` with weight `x^(number of neighbors whose height differs from k)`, then render terraces and contour edges. WITH this constrained law, compare against an unconstrained discrete Gaussian free-field heat-bath sampler matched by block-average variance, as the WITHOUT baseline. Measure maximum neighboring height jump, covariance and histograms of smooth block averages, integrated autocorrelation time, and milliseconds per sweep at several grid sizes.
- Proven vs. speculative: The manuscript proves convergence as random distributions on smooth bounded simply connected domains for each fixed `x` in the stated interval, and gives the exact height and loop laws; source checked in `build/sections/01-introduction.tex`. The local sampler and terrain use are extrapolations: the normalization has no supplied closed formula, no practical sampling-time bound is proved, and the field theorem does not prove a scaling limit for individual contour loops.
- Wow: high · Confidence: med · Effort: M

## G7.4: Show why individually random cards can still form an unfair deck

- Family / paper: 238 — Optimal-order mixing of the Thorp shuffle — `preprints/Optimal-order-mixing-of-the-Thorp-shuffle-September-26-2026/paper.pdf`
- Quote: "We prove that the Thorp shuffle on $2^d$ cards mixes in $\Theta(d)$ complete shuffles."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Animate a switching network for deck order, tournament seeding, or loot-bag permutations: split a power-of-two deck into halves, independently coin-flip the ordering of corresponding pairs, and interleave them. A single card is already uniform after `d=log2(N)` passes, although the whole deck can remain highly nonuniform. The new full-permutation mixing theorem justifies an asymptotic `O(log N)` pass budget for this particular shuffle and supplies the finite support lower bound `TV >= 1-2^(t*N/2)/N!`. The useful lesson is how to test joint fairness; this is not an improvement over exact linear-time Fisher–Yates shuffling.
- Demo sketch: Implement the pair switches in C/C++, animate a larger deck, and separately propagate the exact permutation distribution for `N=8` over its `8!=40320` states. WITH full-deck assessment, show exact total-variation distance and a pass-count curve; WITHOUT, stop after the one-card position histogram becomes uniform at `d` passes. Measure exact small-deck TV, larger-deck pair correlations as partial diagnostics, milliseconds per pass, and total shuffle cost against Fisher–Yates.
- Proven vs. speculative: Source checked in `build/main.tex`: the paper proves worst-start full-law convergence after `1600*d` passes as `d` tends to infinity, plus the all-size support lower bound. The constant is large and not sharp, and the asymptotic upper theorem is not a numerical accuracy guarantee for every finite deck; larger-deck marginal tests cannot certify full TV distance.
- Wow: med · Confidence: high · Effort: M

## G7.5: A disorder slider for lattice-wave interference

- Family / paper: 261 — Pure-Point Spectrum for the Two-Dimensional Anderson Model at Every Positive Disorder — `preprints/Pure-Point-Spectrum-for-the-Two-Dimensional-Anderson-Model-at-Every-Positive-Disorder-September-23-2026/paper.pdf`; companion: Absolutely Continuous Spectrum for Weak-Disorder Anderson Models in Dimensions at Least Three — `preprints/Absolutely-Continuous-Spectrum-for-Weak-Disorder-Anderson-Models-in-Dimensions-at-Least-Three-September-23-2026/paper.pdf`
- Quote: "This resolves the pure-point assertion of the two-dimensional Anderson localization conjecture for the uniform single-site law."
- Kind: visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Make a wave toy with amplitude brightness, phase color, and a slider for independent random site energies. The model is a small nearest-neighbor stencil, `(H*u)(x)=sum_neighbors u(y)+v_x*u(x)`, with `v_x` uniform on `[-h,h]`. The new infinite-volume results give a spectral contrast: every fixed positive `h` in two dimensions produces pure-point spectral type, while sufficiently weak disorder in each fixed dimension at least three leaves a nonzero purely absolutely continuous energy interval. This motivates experiments on trapping and extended states, but supplies no finite-time transport threshold.
- Demo sketch: Evolve a complex packet on a finite square grid in C/C++, using a norm-preserving split of diagonal phase updates and disjoint-edge hopping rotations. WITH random potentials, sweep `h` and several seeds; WITHOUT, use `h=0` on the identical grid and integrator. Record participation ratio, probability near the launch site, mean-square radius before boundary effects dominate, norm drift, and milliseconds per step; optional three-dimensional slices are exploratory rather than theorem-certified weak-disorder settings.
- Proven vs. speculative: Source checked in both introductions and the two-dimensional `build/paper.tex`: the planar theorem proves spectral type, explicitly asserting neither exponential eigenfunction decay nor dynamical localization; the higher-dimensional theorem does not prove diffusion and gives no effective numerical disorder bound. Finite-grid transport, visible trapping scales, and the numerical integrator are extrapolations, and finite matrices alone cannot demonstrate a pure-point-versus-continuous spectral distinction.
- Wow: med · Confidence: med · Effort: M

## G7.6: Size-aware difficulty bands for random logic puzzles

- Family / paper: 235 — Linear Variance of the Random 3-SAT Hitting Time — `preprints/Linear-Variance-of-the-Random-3-SAT-Hitting-Time-October-5-2026/linear-variance-of-the-random-3-sat-hitting-time.pdf`
- Quote: "The upper bound removes the logarithmic loss in the earlier variance estimate; the matching lower bound follows from Wilson's transition-width theorem."
- Kind: gameplay | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Treat Boolean switches as player choices and independently generated three-literal clauses as room, door, or crafting constraints. The first unsatisfiable prefix `H_n` has variance `Theta(n)`, so its standard-deviation scale in clauses is `sqrt(n)` and its density scale is `1/sqrt(n)`. The paper also establishes this order for fixed central satisfiability windows, giving a reason to narrow a difficulty band as puzzle size grows. Calibrate the band's center empirically: this result does not provide a usable numerical 3-SAT threshold or turn clause density into a guarantee of human difficulty.
- Demo sketch: For modest sizes such as `n=10..18`, retain an exact bitset of satisfying assignments and append fair-sign clauses on three distinct variables, sampled with replacement, until the bitset empties. WITH the fluctuation rule, compare offsets of order `sqrt(n)` around the measured transition center; WITHOUT, keep the same density offset across puzzle sizes. Record `Var(H_n)/n`, satisfiability fraction versus offset, central window width divided by `sqrt(n)`, and milliseconds per generated instance.
- Proven vs. speculative: The variance bounds and central-window order are proved for the stated independent random clause process; source checked in `build/source/main.tex`, with constants not numerically optimized. Puzzle difficulty and finite-size calibration are extrapolations, while the exact bitset method costs exponentially in `n`; the family's separate computable-threshold result uses an unbounded certificate search, not a practical game-loop threshold calculator.
- Wow: med · Confidence: med · Effort: S

## G7.7: Noncrossing paths with a calibrated fractal footprint

- Family / paper: 237 — Mass and covering exponents for fixed-length honeycomb walks — `preprints/Mass-and-covering-exponents-for-fixed-length-honeycomb-walks-September-26-2026/main.pdf`
- Quote: "For uniform self-avoiding walks of every sufficiently large integer length on the honeycomb lattice, we prove diameter exponent 3/4 and simultaneous local-mass and covering exponent 4/3, with arbitrary positive exponent slack and arbitrary polynomial failure probability."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Use uniformly sampled fixed-length honeycomb self-avoiding walks as noncrossing tendrils, polymer toys, or winding route layouts. The theorem calibrates their spatial footprint: diameter is `n^(3/4+o(1))`, and local mass and covering counts have exponent `4/3`. This suggests `n` of order `R^(4/3)` when targeting footprint scale `R`, and gives diagnostics for whether a generator actually resembles the intended uniform ensemble. It supplies a new geometric guarantee, not a new fast sampler or a guarantee that the endpoints are far apart.
- Demo sketch: Implement symmetric pivot proposals using lattice-preserving rotations/reflections and reject self-intersections, with exact enumeration at short lengths to check sampling; animate accepted paths. WITH a sampler targeting the uniform fixed-length law, compare against greedy growth among unvisited neighbors with restart on trapping as the WITHOUT baseline. Measure log–log diameter slope, local vertex mass, grid-box covering count as a proxy for ball coverings, pivot acceptance, autocorrelation, and milliseconds per effective sample.
- Proven vs. speculative: Source checked in `build/sections/00_introduction.tex`: the simultaneous estimates hold for every sufficiently large fixed length with arbitrary exponent slack and polynomial failure bounds, but provide no convenient finite cutoff, prefactor, or practical sampler mixing rate. The footprint rule and pivot implementation are extrapolations, and finite chains need bias/autocorrelation checks; neither continuum-curve convergence nor endpoint-separation scaling is asserted.
- Wow: med · Confidence: med · Effort: M

## G7.8: Percolation in a world of binary-tree transformations

- Family / paper: 248 — Thompson's group F is nonamenable — `preprints/Thompsons-group-F-is-nonamenable-September-23-2026/paper.pdf`
- Quote: "Thompson's group \(F\) is not amenable."
- Kind: visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Make locations represent binary-tree transformations, with neighboring locations obtained by fixed generators of Thompson's group, represented as exact dyadic piecewise-linear interval maps. Independently opening movement edges creates percolation on this Cayley graph. The manuscript's consequence of nonamenability guarantees a nonempty probability interval with infinitely many infinite connected regions, suggesting a procedural world in which unbounded regions coexist without joining into one. The bridge is qualitative: it selects an interesting world geometry rather than giving a numerical terrain-generation threshold.
- Demo sketch: In C/C++, build bounded graph balls using canonical reduced tree-pair diagrams or exact dyadic maps, hash equal group elements, label undirected edges once with independent uniforms, and animate increasing opening probability. WITH this theorem-motivated graph, compare cluster behavior against a square lattice under the same opening experiment as the WITHOUT baseline. Across increasing graph radii, measure clusters reaching the outer boundary, interior connectivity, largest-cluster fraction, memory, and milliseconds per update, while displaying the graph cutoff.
- Proven vs. speculative: Source checked in `build/sections/introduction.tex` and `consequences.tex`: the paper proves nonamenability and invokes a companion theorem to obtain `p_c < p_(2->2) <= p_u` for every finite symmetric generating set, including a simultaneously nonunique interval in the shared-label coupling. Thresholds are not numeric, graph balls grow rapidly, and finite boundary-reaching clusters cannot certify infinite clusters; the visualization is exploratory, with no practical expansion certificate or performance gain promised.
- Wow: med · Confidence: low · Effort: L
