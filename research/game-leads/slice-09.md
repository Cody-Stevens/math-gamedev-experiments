## G9.1: Median-of-three diffusion for graph-driven game attributes
- Family / paper: 332 — Metric Markov Cotype Two of ℓ₁ — `preprints/Metric-Markov-Cotype-Two-of-l1-October-5-2026/build/main.tex`
- Quote: "The modified values are not prescribed linear averages."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Give graph nodes real attribute vectors $x_i$ representing biome properties, faction influence, or NPC preferences, and measure changes with the ℓ₁ norm. For a stochastic transition matrix $A$ with stationary distribution $\pi$ and integer horizon $t\ge1$, set $p=1/(t+1)$, $q=1-p$, and $G=p(I-qA)^{-1}$; the new value $y_i$ is the expected coordinatewise median of three independent input vectors sampled from row $G_{i,*}$. Compute this expectation exactly by sorting each coordinate's distinct values $v_0<\cdots<v_m$: with $h_{i,\ell}=\sum_{j:x_j(k)\ge v_\ell}G_{ij}$, use $y_i(k)=v_0+\sum_{\ell=1}^m(v_\ell-v_{\ell-1})(3h_{i,\ell}^2-2h_{i,\ell}^3)$. The horizon controls a geometric walk of mean length $t$, while the cubic pushes binary threshold probabilities above one-half upward and those below one-half downward.
- Demo sketch: A small C/C++ demo colors a 64–128-node grid or irregular graph by three independent attribute channels and exposes a horizon slider. WITH uses the exact median construction; WITHOUT uses the linear geometric average $Gx$ with the same transition matrix and horizon. Record movement cost $M=\sum_i\pi_i\|x_i-y_i\|_1^2$, edge energy $E=\sum_{i,j}\pi_i a_{ij}\|y_i-y_j\|_1^2$, $(M+tE)/W$ where $W=\sum_{i,j}\pi_i[(1/t)\sum_{s=1}^tA^s]_{ij}\|x_i-x_j\|_1^2$, boundary contrast, and milliseconds per update.
- Proven vs. speculative: The paper proves $M+tE\le3024W$ for its explicit construction, independently of graph size, coordinate count, and horizon; the theorem states reversibility, and its stationary-chain remark confirms that stationarity suffices. Better-looking biome boundaries are speculative, the constant 3024 is loose, and there is no speed guarantee or preservation of total resource mass or probability-simplex constraints; Monte Carlo approximations would need separate error bounds.
- Wow: med · Confidence: high · Effort: S

## G9.2: A factor-two error certificate for coupled simulation filters
- Family / paper: 325 — The complete Crouzeix theorem: optimal similarity and a common positive boundary representation — `preprints/The-complete-Crouzeix-theorem-September-23-2026/build/main.tex`; previous-bound comparison checked in A direct proof of the complete Crouzeix inequality — `preprints/A-direct-proof-of-the-complete-Crouzeix-inequality-September-26-2026/build/main.tex`
- Quote: "We resolve the complete Crouzeix conjecture by proving the sharp constant-two numerical-range inequality for every bounded operator on a complex Hilbert space and every matrix-valued polynomial."
- Kind: performance | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Certify polynomial approximations used to evolve a linear simulation or apply a coupled multichannel filter, including directional interactions represented by nonnormal matrices. For $P(z)=\sum_kB_kz^k$, the result gives $\|\sum_kA^k\otimes B_k\|\le2\max_{z\in W(A)}\|P(z)\|$, where $W(A)=\{u^*Au:\|u\|_2=1\}$, and the companion's holomorphic extension covers errors such as $e^{\tau z}-P(z)$. An approximation error at most $\varepsilon$ throughout the numerical range therefore gives operator error at most $2\varepsilon$, rather than relying on eigenvalues that can miss nonnormal amplification. The sharp factor improves the previous complete bound $1+\sqrt2$ recorded in the direct paper, allowing a less conservative polynomial degree when the error tolerance crosses a degree-selection threshold.
- Demo sketch: Use a 32–64-cell directional chain $A=-aI+kS$, with $S$ the nilpotent one-step shift, and animate its response to an impulse; its exact exponential is the finite sum $e^{-a\tau}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(k\tau)^jS^j/j!$. WITH selects the Taylor degree using the factor-two certificate; WITHOUT uses $1+\sqrt2$ with the same rigorous scalar error estimate on a disk enclosing $W(A)$. Record selected degree, matrix-vector products, milliseconds per update, and actual error against the exact reference, including parameter sweeps where both bounds select the same degree.
- Proven vs. speculative: The dimension-independent factor two, its sharpness, and the holomorphic extension are proved; using them to choose game-loop approximation degrees is an extrapolation, with a modest possible saving rather than a new integrator. A sampled numerical-range boundary alone is not a rigorous supremum bound, and this operator estimate supplies neither nonlinear stability nor control of accumulated roundoff or repeated-step error.
- Wow: med · Confidence: high · Effort: M

## G9.3: Hierarchical ability budgets from two recursive fields
- Family / paper: 331 — Midpoint convexity from two recursive potentials — `preprints/Midpoint-convexity-from-two-recursive-potentials-September-27-2026/build/sections/foundations.tex`; finite tail-energy proof checked in `preprints/Midpoint-convexity-from-two-recursive-potentials-September-27-2026/build/sections/energy.tex`
- Quote: "Only the finite predecessor closure of the support has nonzero fields."
- Kind: gameplay | visualization | sim-design-rule
- Idea: Assign signed values $v_s$ to a branching ability tree or procedural hierarchy and charge a cost that lets aligned demands share capacity while opposing signs require separate capacity. Evaluate nodes from leaves to root with $A_s=(\sum_cP_c^2)^{1/2}$, $B_s=(\sum_cQ_c^2)^{1/2}$, $P_s=\max\{A_s,v_s+B_s\}$, and $Q_s=\max\{B_s,-v_s+A_s\}$, using zero fields below the support. In the paper's joined forest with fixed zero joining coordinate $\rho$, the budget is $N_0(v)=P_\rho(v)=Q_\rho(v)$, computable in one pass over the finite predecessor closure. The construction gives drawable, hierarchy-dependent budget balls, and restricting coordinates to a set containing all their ancestors cannot increase the budget.
- Demo sketch: Animate signed node values, the propagated $P/Q$ fields, and two-dimensional slices of the budget ball in a small C/C++ tree editor. WITH evaluates this two-field norm and shows the cost of paired edits $x+z$ and $x-z$ in descendants outside an existing head; WITHOUT uses the conventional scalar recursion $C_s=|v_s|+(\sum_cC_c^2)^{1/2}$. Measure microseconds per evaluation as the tree grows, budget differences, midpoint excess against the explicit lower bound, and the budget ratio before and after ancestor-closed truncation.
- Proven vs. speculative: The fields are the least feasible nonnegative pair, truncation is contractive, and for $x$ supported on a finite ancestor-closed head $H$ and nonzero $z$ vanishing on $H$, the paper proves $[N_0(x+z)+N_0(x-z)]/2-N_0(x)\ge N_0(z)^3/[8(2N_0(x)+N_0(z))^2]$. Ability balancing is speculative and no advantage over the scalar baseline is proved; a finite demo can verify the recursion and tail inequality but cannot establish the paper's infinite-dimensional obstruction to alternative convex renormings.
- Wow: med · Confidence: high · Effort: S
